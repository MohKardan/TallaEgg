## 6. High-Priority Findings

### [H-9] Missing Authorization Checks on Callback Queries (IDOR)
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | High |
| **Location** | `TelegramBot\TallaEgg.TelegramBot.Infrastructure\BotHandler.cs` (`HandleCallbackQueryAsync` lines 667-685, 748-765) |
| **Evidence** | The bot parses `orders_{userId}` and `cancel_order_{orderId}` callback prefixes, extracts the GUID, and directly passes it to `GetUserOrdersAsync` and `CancelOrderAsync` respectively, without asserting that the `CallbackQuery.From.Id` belongs to the identity bound to the GUID. |
| **Failure Scenario** | An attacker intercepts or enumerates a target's order GUID. They send a Telegram callback containing `data = "cancel_order_<target_order_id>"`. The Bot intercepts this and blindly cancels the victim's order. |
| **Reproduction** | Attempted to mock Telegram webhook payloads but required excessive harness setup for the bot. Finding was confirmed exclusively by tracing the execution path in C#. Confidence lowered to Medium per methodology v10. |
| **Analysis** | Relying purely on the unguessability of GUIDs for authorization is an incomplete defense. If a GUID is leaked or guessed, an attacker possesses total control over the referenced resource. |
| **Risk** | Business consequence: Unauthorized cancellation of pending orders disrupting trade execution. Engineering consequence: Breach of zero-trust boundaries at the gateway. |
| **Recommendation** | In `HandleCallbackQueryAsync`, fetch the order via `orderId` and assert that the `UserId` of the order matches the logged-in user mapped to the `telegramId` initiating the callback. |
| **Confidence** | Medium (Inferred from trace, not executed) |
| **Estimated Effort** | Low |
| **Already Tracked** | New (Searched: `gh issue list --state all --search "orders OR IDOR OR cancel OR auth OR callback"`) |

## 8. Key Positive Findings

* **Data Layer Concurrency:** The infrastructure gracefully manages contention. `WalletConcurrencyTests` confirms that `SettleTradeAsync` and `LockBalanceAsync` correctly employ `WithConcurrencyRetryAsync` to resolve EF Core optimistic concurrency exceptions securely.
* **Database Migrations:** The API seamlessly acquires exclusive SQL locks (`sp_getapplock` on `__EFMigrationsLock`) during application startup to orchestrate migrations reliably without race conditions.

## 9. Security Assessment

**Score: 5 / 10**
The gateway layer (Telegram Bot) has insufficient defensive programming against IDOR (H-9), relying heavily on the unguessability of GUIDs rather than explicitly verifying ownership. Furthermore, M-5 and M-2 (Auth & Rate Limiting) remain unresolved.

## 10. Financial & Data Integrity Assessment

**Score: 5 / 10**
Capped at 5/10 due to unresolved H-6. The Time-Of-Check-To-Time-Of-Use (TOCTOU) problem during order confirmation allows users to bypass their unified credit limit because the atomic database lock operates on individual assets without evaluating the cross-asset credit ceiling simultaneously.

## 11. Architecture Assessment

**Score: 8 / 10**
The fundamental microservices layout and the separation of concerns between Orders, Wallet, and the Bot remain clean. The outbox patterns reliably prevent data loss across service boundaries.
